INTRODUCTION

What is Electromagnetism?

Electromagnetism is a fundamental branch of physics that studies the interaction between electric charges, electric fields and magnetic fields. It explains everything from the interaction of charged particles to the propagation of light.

CHAPTER 01

Electrostatics

The study of electric charges at rest and the fields produced by them.

Coulomb's Law

The electrostatic force between two point charges is proportional to the product of their charges and inversely proportional to the square of their separation.

F = (1 / 4πε₀) × |q₁q₂| / r²

Electric Field

Electric field is the force experienced per unit positive test charge.

E = F / q

Gauss's Law

The total electric flux through a closed surface depends on the enclosed charge.

∮ E · dA = Q / ε₀

Electric Potential

Electric potential is the work done per unit charge in bringing a test charge from infinity.

V = W / q

CHAPTER 02

Magnetostatics

Study magnetic fields produced by steady electric currents.

Biot-Savart Law

Determines the magnetic field produced by a small current element.

dB = (μ₀ / 4π) × (I dl × r̂) / r²

Ampere's Law

The circulation of the magnetic field around a closed loop is related to the enclosed current.

∮ B · dl = μ₀ I

Lorentz Force

The force on a charged particle moving in electric and magnetic fields.

F = q(E + v × B)

Magnetic Flux

Magnetic flux measures the magnetic field passing through a given surface.

ΦB = ∫ B · dA

CHAPTER 03

Electromagnetic Induction

Faraday's Law

An induced EMF is produced by a changing magnetic flux through a circuit.

ε = -dΦB / dt

Lenz's Law

The induced current opposes the change in magnetic flux that produces it.

ε = -N dΦB / dt

CHAPTER 04

Maxwell's Equations

Four fundamental equations that unify electricity, magnetism and optics.

1. Gauss's Law for Electricity

∇ · E = ρ / ε₀

Electric charges act as sources of electric fields.

2. Gauss's Law for Magnetism

∇ · B = 0

There are no isolated magnetic monopoles in classical electromagnetism.

3. Faraday's Law

∇ × E = -∂B / ∂t

A changing magnetic field produces an electric field.

4. Ampere-Maxwell Law

∇ × B = μ₀J + μ₀ε₀ ∂E / ∂t

Electric current and changing electric fields produce magnetic fields.

CHAPTER 05

Electromagnetic Waves

Electromagnetic waves are self-propagating disturbances of electric and magnetic fields.

Wave Speed

The speed of electromagnetic waves in vacuum.

c = 1 / √(μ₀ε₀)

Wave Relation

The relation between frequency, wavelength and wave speed.

c = νλ

Poynting Vector

Represents electromagnetic energy flow per unit area per unit time.

S = (1 / μ₀)(E × B)

Energy Density

Total electric and magnetic energy stored per unit volume.

u = ½ε₀E² + B²/(2μ₀)
